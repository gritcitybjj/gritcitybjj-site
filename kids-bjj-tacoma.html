Parents looking for kids jiu-jitsu in Tacoma can start at GRIT CITY BJJ. The kids program is for ages 5 and up. Classes meet at Cedar Plaza, 3630 S Cedar St, STE L, Tacoma, WA 98409. The academy has been Teaching Tacoma Since 2011, and the first class is free.

Confidence, discipline, and anti-bullying

The kids class is built around three things families ask for: confidence, discipline, and anti-bullying. Students learn how to stay calm, how to listen, and how to handle pressure without panicking. The No Bullies message is part of how class is run. Children practice respectful posture, a clean uniform, and the habit of addressing coaches properly. Those habits are meant to show up at school and at home, not only on the mat.

Anti-bullying work here is not a separate lecture. Students learn that size is not permission, that they can speak up, and that they can step away and find a coach. They also learn that jiu-jitsu is not something to use to impress or intimidate anyone at school. The code of conduct applies to kids the same way it applies to adults: be respectful, be humble, and keep the training on the mats.

What a kids class is like

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu for kids at this academy is real martial arts taught at a child's pace. A class follows the same shape as the adult curriculum, scaled down. There is a warm-up for movement and coordination, step-by-step technique, and controlled partner work. Live training stays supervised. Children are not thrown into hard sparring on day one.

The goal of the first month is comfort on the mat. Kids learn names, learn how to fall and stand safely, and learn one or two positions well enough to recognize them. Parents often notice a child who was shy at the door start to walk in ready to bow onto the mat. Focus during instruction gets longer. Coordination improves because the warm-up repeats the same movement patterns. A child does not need to be athletic before they start. The class is where the fitness and the focus come from.

Safety and coaches

Safety is part of the room. Shoes stay off the mats. Nails are trimmed. Jewelry stays off the mat. Water is in a bottle. Visitors sit in the spectator area and do not coach from the side. If a student is late, they wait outside until a coach invites them in.

The coaches on the mat are Professor Marcello C. Monteiro, Professor Michael Proctor Sr., Coach Donnie McMasters, and Coach Chris Kuykendall. They teach the lineage Mitsuyo Maeda > Carlos Gracie Sr. > Carlson Gracie Sr. > Ricardo De La Riva > Marcello C. Questions parents ask

  • Does my child have to compete? No. Competition is optional for students who want it.
  • What if my child is nervous? That is normal. The free class is a chance to see the room, meet the coach, and leave whenever you need to.
  • Can I watch? Yes. Spectators stay in the designated seating, keep devices quiet, and leave the coaching to the coaches.
  • What should we do after class? If it felt right, ask about membership. If you need a night to talk it over, call the next day.

Adults who want to train too can read the adult Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu page. A lot of families start with a child and then a parent joins.
